      It&#8217;s October, it&#8217;s still Breast Cancer Awareness Month, although Secondary Breast Cancer Awareness Day has been and gone&#8230; but we&#8217;re still very much living with that reality every day. [<img src="/media/2020/09/31-landscape-1024x819.jpg" alt="Infographic - 31 people die every day in the UK" width="640" height="512" loading="lazy" decoding="async">](/media/2020/09/31-landscape.jpg)

      The focus this year for us is the [#IAmThe31 campaign](/i-am-the-31/), reflecting on the 31 people who die every day from secondary breast cancer (11,500 women and 80 men per year in the UK alone)&#8230; many of us have the joy of getting to know people within social media spaces, but then watch our friends dying on a frequent basis &#8211; so we want to [do something about it](/aims-and-objectives/), and would appreciate your support (<a href="https://www.gofundme.com/f/metupuk" rel="noopener" target="_blank">financial</a>, [social media shares](/help-us/), offering content/ideas).

      A number of us set ourselves challenges to do something related to the number 31 (or 3.1) &#8211; all challenges according to our own abilities. We hope this will encourage more awareness of what it&#8217;s like to live with secondary breast cancer, remember those who have already died &#8211; and what we can do to make that time longer/better quality of life for those of us still living with it&#8230; whilst helping our own physical and mental health.

      ## Bex Lewis (<a href="https://twitter.com/drbexl" rel="noopener" target="_blank">@drbexl</a>)

      Bex was hospitalised in July with tumours pressing on her main veins/blocked artery as a treatment side effect. As someone who used to cycle to/from work, go to the gym, hike at the weekends, loves wild swimming, and has run 10ks &#8211; it&#8217;s been strange not being able to walk far. August she managed around 1km per day average, September around 2.2km, so decided to push for 3.1km per day average for October (bearing in mind treatment every Monday is typically about 1km). If you donated 31p for 31p days, that would be <a href="https://www.gofundme.com/f/metupuk" rel="noopener" target="_blank">£9.61 to METUPUK.</a>

      https://drbexl.co.uk/2020/10/16/cancer-ongoing-update-and-why-am-i-walking-average-3-1km-per-day-iamthe31-busylivingwithmets/

      ## Jo Taylor (<a href="https://twitter.com/abcdiagnosis" rel="noopener" target="_blank">@abcdiagnosis</a>)

      Jo is the founder of METUP UK, and runs <a href="https://www.abcdiagnosis.co.uk/lifestyle/abcd-retreat/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ABCD exercise retreats</a> to encourage breast cancer patients to find a way to get moving again after or alongside treatment. Super fit, Jo has had a lot of treatment this year, which has interfered with her ability to get out and about &#8211; but for the rest of October is walking 3.1 miles per day, and hoping to raise £310 for METUP &#8211; asking 100 people to <a href="https://www.gofundme.com/f/metupuk" rel="noopener" target="_blank">donate £3.10</a>.

      Earlier this year, Jo undertook activities around 3.1. She wasn&#8217;t even that she would make it to October, and spent much of that much hospitalised, with friends picking up the baton and undertaking her 3.1 miles (walking, running, rowing) each day.
