      ### If you are worried about a symptom

      The red flag infographics above set out where secondary breast cancer most often spreads — bone, lung, liver, brain, lymph nodes, and spinal cord compression — and what it can feel like.

      **If a symptom has lasted more than three weeks, contact your medical team.** You do not need to wait for your next appointment, and you do not need to be certain it is something. Ask to be seen.

      There is more on the infographics, and how to use them, on our [red flag infographic page](/about-us/red-flag-sbcinfographic/).
  - _template: "disclosure"
    summary: "If you have just been diagnosed"
    body: |-
      A secondary diagnosis is not the same conversation as a primary one, and a lot of the information you will find online is written for primary breast cancer. These are the places we send people first:

      - **[Breast Cancer Now](https://breastcancernow.org)** — a nurse helpline, and a Living with Secondary Breast Cancer service that runs monthly meet-ups across the UK and online.
      - **[Macmillan Cancer Support](https://www.macmillan.org.uk)** — practical and financial help, including benefits advice and help with work.
      - **[Secondary1st](https://secondary1st.org.uk)** — a UK charity funding research specifically into secondary breast cancer.
      - **[ABC Diagnosis](https://abcdiagnosis.co.uk)** — our founder Jo Taylor's site, where the red flag infographics began.

      You can also [join us](/help-us/join-us/). Most of our members came to METUPUK in the months after their own diagnosis.
  - _template: "disclosure"
    summary: "Treatment and drug access"
    body: |-
      Whether a drug is available to you depends on where you live in the UK and on decisions made by bodies most people have never heard of until they need one.

      - **[NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk)** — decides which treatments the NHS in England funds, and publishes its reasoning. The Scottish Medicines Consortium does the same for Scotland.
      - **Our own coverage** — we have written about most of the major decisions as they happened, including Trodelvy, Enhertu and tucatinib. Start at [research and trials](/research-and-trials/), or browse the [drug approval](/news/topic/drug-approval/) and [treatment options](/news/topic/treatment-options/) topics.

      If a treatment you need is not funded where you live, that is something we campaign on. Tell us.
  - _template: "disclosure"
    summary: "Clinical trials"
    body: |-
      - **[Be Part of Research](https://bepartofresearch.nihr.ac.uk)** — the NIHR's search for trials recruiting in the UK. You can search by condition and location.
      - **[Cancer Research UK](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org)** — a trials database written for patients rather than clinicians.

      Ask your oncologist about trials even if they have not raised it. Our [clinical trial](/news/topic/clinical-trial/) posts include members writing about what taking part was actually like.
